Create a single portrait illustration for record 18, “Qing Ping Le: Liupan Mountain” (1935), in the visual language of a Chinese revolutionary-era woodcut print. Capture the poem’s summit clarity, the vast completed distance, and the question of the future through landscape and gesture rather than a literal story.

A small group of Red Army climbers reaches the steep, windswept summit of Liupan Mountain. Their uniforms and any horse are neutral charcoal, grey, and warm paper, never solid red. One single vermilion flag streams horizontally in a forceful west wind, carving the principal red accent into the pale high sky. A long pale trail spirals upward through layered ranges from the lower foreground, visually suggesting twenty thousand li without turning into a diagram. Far south, a V of migrating geese crosses a high open field of clouds. In the summit foreground, one figure holds an unspooled length of dark rope that arcs beyond the peak toward a distant, sinuous Great Wall-like ridge and dark dragon-shaped storm cloud, making “the long cord in hand” a restrained visual metaphor; the rope is not visibly tied and the dragon remains only a cloud form, not a literal creature. The mood is clear, elevated, expectant, and unresolved, with immense distance beyond the finished climb.
