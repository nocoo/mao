Create a single portrait illustration for record 23, “Lang Tao Sha: Beidaihe” (1954), in the visual language of an original Chinese revolutionary-era woodcut print. Convey the poem’s stormy sea, vanished fishing boat, ancient memory, and newly changed human world through one coastal landscape rather than a historical reenactment of Cao Cao.

From a high dark promontory, look out over the rain-blackened Bohai Sea. White breakers rise like carved ridges beneath a vast slanting curtain of rain, and a single tiny traditional fishing boat is almost swallowed at the edge of visibility. The low Qinhuang Island coast threads through mist at one side, while a weathered stone headland in the middle distance suggests the long-submerged Jieshi landscape without inventing a named monument. Wind tears sheets of grey cloud across the water and bends a few shore pines; a restrained red autumn-leaf branch and a small break in the storm carry the sense of time passing from ancient winds to the present. The atmosphere is stormy, reflective, and forceful, with no identifiable historical figure on the shore.

Use bold hand-carved shapes, expressive gouge marks, rough ink impressions, layered printmaking textures, and a limited palette of revolutionary vermilion red, ink black, warm paper white, and muted grey. Reserve red for scattered windblown leaves and a narrow weather accent, allowing black surf and spacious white foam to dominate. Original artwork, not a reproduction of a historical print, with large wave masses and fine rain, stone, and pine detail.
