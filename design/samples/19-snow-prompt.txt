Create a single portrait illustration for the poem “Qinyuanchun: Snow” (1936), in the visual language of a Chinese revolutionary-era woodcut print. The image should express the poem through landscape, not illustrate it literally line by line.

Look across an immense northern winter landscape from a high vantage point. A snow-covered Great Wall winds over distant ridges and disappears into the white horizon. Below, a broad frozen Yellow River curves silently through the land. Sweeping mountain ridges move like silver serpents; the rounded, snow-covered highlands suggest a herd in motion through their shapes alone, with no literal snakes or elephants. The sky has just cleared after a snowfall. A deep vermilion sun and a few restrained red accents illuminate the white land, expressing “red attire and white wrapping.” The scene feels expansive, vigorous, forward-looking, and full of resolve.

Use bold hand-carved shapes, expressive gouge marks, rough ink impressions, layered printmaking textures, and a carefully limited palette of revolutionary vermilion red, ink black, warm paper white, and muted grey. Strong contrast and a dynamic diagonal composition, with large readable forms and fine details that reward closer viewing. The snow must remain bright and spacious; red is powerful but does not overwhelm the landscape. Original artwork, not a reproduction of a historical print.

Vertical composition for a 3:4 display frame. The native image may be taller: keep the main landscape, sun, Great Wall, and frozen river inside the central 3:4 area, allowing the top and bottom edges to be cropped. No lettering, calligraphy, caption, watermark, border, portrait, literal animals, modern structures, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
