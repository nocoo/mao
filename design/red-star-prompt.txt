The visual language is that of Chinese hand-painted propaganda posters and painted film posters from approximately 1966–1975: disciplined monumental composition, decisive vermilion red shapes, warm ivory and ochre light, deep forest-green shadows, skillful opaque gouache and lithographic color separation. It should look like a masterful painted print, NOT a flat vector icon, NOT contemporary 3D rendering, and NOT a photorealistic photograph.

Composition: a single magnificent five-pointed red star occupies the upper-middle quarter of the painting, with a bright, restrained gold-white glint on one facet and strong red planar shading. The star feels like an emblem painted into the scene, not a futuristic floating object. Long confident golden sunbeams radiate behind it across a warm cream sky. A large red silk flag sweeps upward diagonally behind the star; the flag has NO writing or symbols. The bottom half opens onto an epic Chinese landscape: the layered peaks and cliffs of Jinggangshan, winding pale river, a path cutting through dense deep green pine trees and sunlit ochre hills. A few very small human silhouettes walking together on the distant mountain path may provide scale, but no portrait and no crowd. Warm, hopeful, energetic, stately; the landscape and red star are the visual subject. Strong asymmetry, sweeping movement, meticulously controlled hand-painted details. Crimson, deep green, warm yellow, ink-black shadows and ivory paper form a coherent restricted palette. Organic brush marks and convincing printed paint, not a uniform artificial grain filter. No glossy game-art look. Keep the star and the main landforms away from the outermost edges so the composition survives slight responsive cropping.
